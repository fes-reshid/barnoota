Forty years under one tree

The Australian Oromo Community Association in Victoria has been the gathering place of Oromo people in Melbourne since 1984 — through new arrivals, new generations and a great deal of coffee.

Our story

It started with a handful of families and a hall

In 1984 a small group of Oromo people living in Melbourne began meeting to keep their language alive, mark their festivals and help one another find work and housing. They formalised that into an association so it would outlast any one of them. It has.

Since then, every wave of Oromo arrivals to Victoria has found the same thing waiting: someone who speaks Afaan Oromoo, knows how the system works, and has already put the coffee on. Our members are students, nurses, drivers, traders, parents, grandparents — people who build Victoria during the week and build this community on the weekend.

We are not a service that is done to our community. We are our community, organising itself.

  • 1984

    The association is founded by Oromo families in Melbourne — the date still printed on our emblem.

  • 1990s – 2000s

    Growing arrivals from Oromia bring new families. Settlement support, weekend language classes and cultural celebrations become regular fixtures.

  • 2000

    The association's ABN is registered, putting our governance and reporting on a formal footing.

  • 2021

    AOCAV is registered as a charity with the ACNC, opening the door to grant-funded community programs.

  • Today

    Weekly programs in Footscray and Mount Cottrell, hundreds of members, and a graduation ceremony that fills the hall every December.

Odaa — the sycamore fig The tree on our emblem is the Odaa, where Oromo assemblies have met for centuries to speak as equals. Shade for everyone, roots that hold.
Why we exist

Our purpose, in plain words

01 Walitti dhufeenya

Belonging

That every Oromo person in Victoria — newly arrived or Melbourne-born — has a community that knows their name.

02 Aadaa

Culture

That Afaan Oromoo, Gadaa values, Irreecha, music, dress and the coffee ceremony are living practice here, not memory.

03 Guddina

Advancement

That our young people finish school, go further, and that our families are healthy, housed, employed and heard.

Our mission

To bring Oromo people in Victoria together; to preserve and share Oromo language, culture and heritage; to support families to settle, learn and succeed in Australia; and to represent our community's voice fairly and respectfully wherever decisions about us are made.

Our promise

Our programs are free or low-cost. Our doors are open to every Oromo person regardless of region, clan, faith or politics — and to every Australian who wants to know us. What is said at our help desk stays with our help desk.

How we govern

Gadaa, in a modern association

Gadaa is the Oromo system of governance by consultation, shared responsibility and the peaceful handover of leadership — one of the world's oldest democratic traditions. Our constitution is Australian; our instincts are Oromo.

We meet, then decide

Significant decisions go to the members. Our annual general meeting elects the committee and sets direction.

Leadership rotates

Office-bearers serve a term and hand over. Experience stays in the community, not in one person's hands.

Elders are consulted

Our elders hold the history and the protocol. On matters of culture and reconciliation, we listen to them first.

We account for ourselves

As an incorporated association and registered charity we report publicly, and members can ask us anything.
