Take your place in the community

A community association is only as strong as the people who turn up for it. Here are four ways to be part of ours — all of them welcome, none of them complicated.

Membership

Belong formally, vote at the AGM and help steer the association.

Volunteering

Teach, tutor, drive, cook, interpret, coach or help run an event.

Partnerships

For councils, schools, health services, funders and businesses.

Support us

In-kind gifts, venue space, equipment or a financial contribution.
Membership

Become a member of AOCAV

Membership is open to Oromo people living in Victoria and to anyone who supports our purpose. Members are the association — you elect the committee, vote on direction at the annual general meeting, and get first word on everything we run.

  • A vote at the annual general meeting and the right to stand for the committee
  • Free enrolment for your children in Afaan Oromoo Saturday school
  • Early notice and member pricing for the cultural night and community dinners
  • Support from the community in times of celebration and of loss

Membership fees are set by the members at the AGM and are kept deliberately low. No one is turned away because of cost — talk to us in confidence.

Volunteering

Give a few hours. Change a family's year.

You do not need qualifications or perfect English. You need to be reliable and kind. Everything else we will show you.

Barsiisaa

Teach or tutor

Afaan Oromoo classes, homework help, English conversation practice, or help with maths and science for high schoolers.

Qajeelchaa

Mentor a young person

Meet a young person once or twice a month. Talk about study, work, driving lessons, life. It matters more than you think.

Hiikaa

Interpret & translate

Sit with families at the help desk, translate letters and forms, or help us produce information in Afaan Oromoo.

Nyaata

Cook & host

Prepare the coffee ceremony, cook for a gathering, set up and pack down. The food is half of why people come.

Geejjiba

Drive & deliver

Bring an elder to an appointment or to Elders Voices, or move chairs, tables and equipment before an event.

Hojii

Skills & admin

Bookkeeping, grant writing, photography, social media, website updates, first aid, coaching. Tell us what you've got.

Offer your time

Volunteers working with children will be asked for a Working with Children Check — we will help you apply, and it is free for volunteers.

Partnerships

Work with us, not around us

Councils, schools, health services, universities, police, housing providers and funders all want to reach the Oromo community well. We are the door — and we will tell you honestly what will work and what will not.

We can offer:

  • Trusted reach into Oromo households across Melbourne's west and north
  • Cultural guidance on language, protocol, gender, religion and regional sensitivities
  • Venues and audiences for information sessions, health clinics and consultations
  • Delivery capability as an incorporated association and registered charity with grant experience

Support our work

What keeps the lights on

Our programs are free because people give. Here is exactly what help looks like for us.

Food & coffee

Coffee, sugar, incense, bread and ingredients for our gatherings — or a catering contribution for a big event.

Space & equipment

A hall for a Saturday, chairs and tables, a PA system, laptops for the homework club, sports gear for the youth teams.

Financial support

Sponsor the graduation ceremony, fund a term of Afaan Oromoo school, or make a general contribution to our programs.

Before you give

AOCAV is a registered charity but is not currently endorsed as a deductible gift recipient, which means donations to us are not tax-deductible. We would rather you knew that up front.

We do not take payments through this website. To arrange a contribution, sponsorship or in-kind donation, contact the committee directly and we will give you our banking details and a receipt.

Talk to us about giving

Not sure where you fit? Just come along.

Turn up to the next gathering, have a coffee, meet a few people. You will work out the rest from there — that is how most of our volunteers started.
