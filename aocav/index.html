Who we are

A community association built by Oromo families, for Oromo families.

AOCAV was founded in 1984 by Oromo people who had made Victoria their home and wanted somewhere their children could hear their language, their elders could be honoured, and newly arrived families would never have to work things out alone.

Four decades later that is still exactly what we do. We are a not-for-profit incorporated association and a registered charity, run by our own members — volunteers who give their Saturdays to teach, drive, cook, translate, mentor and organise.

  • Culture that lasts — Afaan Oromoo, Irreecha, buna ceremonies, music and dress passed to the next generation.
  • Young people who thrive — mentoring, study support and leadership, celebrated every year at our graduation ceremony.
  • Nobody left behind — practical settlement help in our own language, from Centrelink forms to school enrolments.

What we do

Six ways we hold the community together

Everything we run comes from the same place — people in our community saw a need, and we built something to meet it.

Education & graduation

Afaan Oromoo Saturday school, homework help and study support — and the annual graduation ceremony where the whole community celebrates every student who finishes.

Youth & mentoring

Leadership circles, sport, career nights and one-to-one mentoring that keep young Oromo-Australians in school, in work and proud of who they are.

Elders & wellbeing

Elders Voices gatherings, coffee mornings, health information in Afaan Oromoo and transport help, so our older community members stay connected and well.

Culture & language

Irreecha, cultural nights, traditional dance and dress, oral history and the Gadaa values that have guided Oromo life for generations.

Women & families

The Women's Buna & Wellbeing Circle, English conversation practice, parenting support and a safe space built on the Oromo women's tradition of siinqee.

Settlement & advocacy

Our weekly help desk with forms, housing, Medicare and referrals — and a clear community voice to councils, government and service providers.

Our proudest day

The graduation ceremony that brings everyone together

Once a year the hall fills with gowns, caps, ululation and the smell of roasting coffee. Year 12 students, apprentices, TAFE and university graduates, and the children who finished a year of Afaan Oromoo school all walk up together while the elders bless them and every family in the room claps.

For parents who arrived in this country with nothing, watching their child cross that stage is the moment all of it makes sense. That is why we never let a graduate go uncelebrated — if someone in your family finished something this year, we want their name on the list.

How we work

Guided by Gadaa

Gadaa is the Oromo system of governance by consultation and rotation — one of the oldest democratic traditions in the world. Its principles still shape how this association makes decisions.

ᛝNagaa

Peace

We build calm, respectful spaces where every family feels safe to speak.

ᛝWalii-galtee

Consensus

Decisions are reached by talking until the community agrees — not by shortcuts.

ᛝSafuu

Ethics

We hold ourselves to the Oromo moral order: honesty, dignity and knowing one's duty.

ᛝAraara

Reconciliation

When there is conflict, elders bring people back together. Nobody is written off.
