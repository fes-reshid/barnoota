What this protects, honestly

  • Every browser and app on this PC resolves through the Noor Shield service, so blocking isn't limited to one browser.
  • Protection runs as a Windows service, independently of this window. Closing or quitting the app does not stop it — it keeps running after logoff and across reboots, and restarts itself automatically if it crashes.
  • A user with their own administrator account can still stop or remove the service. This raises the barrier; it isn't an unbreakable lock. Give the child a standard (non-admin) Windows account.
  • Sites reached by raw IP address, or through a browser using its own encrypted DNS, can bypass domain filtering.
  • A blocked site shows a Hadith reminder page instead of a broken connection, on both http:// and https:// — see below for how.

How the reminder page works on https:// sites

Showing a proper page (instead of a certificate warning) on an https:// site needs a small local certificate authority, created once on this PC and added to Windows' list of trusted authorities. It's used for exactly one thing: signing a certificate for whatever blocked site your browser tried to reach, so it can show our reminder page instead of a warning. It cannot see or intercept traffic for any site that isn't already blocked — nothing else on this PC is decrypted or inspected. Removing protection (Parent settings) removes it from Windows too.

About virus protection

Noor Shield blocks known malware-distribution and phishing domains — it will stop this PC from reaching a site already known to spread malware or steal passwords. It is not antivirus software: it cannot scan files already on this PC, remove an existing infection, or watch running programs. Windows' own built-in Microsoft Defender handles that, and should stay turned on alongside this.
