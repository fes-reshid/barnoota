Help & Tutorials

Everything the audio editor can do, explained step by step — from opening your first file to splitting a recitation into lines, cleaning up a recording and saving MP3s.

Contents

Getting started

A full audio editor that runs in your web browser — on a computer, tablet or phone. There is nothing to install and no account.

Private by design. Your recordings never leave your device: opening, editing, effects and saving all happen inside the browser. Nothing is uploaded.

What you can open and save

  • Open: MP3, WAV, M4A/AAC, OGG, WebM, FLAC — and the sound track of most video files. You can open many files at once; each gets its own tab.
  • Save: MP3 (small, plays everywhere), OGG/Opus (smallest — what WhatsApp uses), M4A (iPhone-friendly, where the browser supports it), FLAC (lossless, about half the size of WAV) or WAV. Parts and batches are saved together in a .zip.
  • Tags: press Tags… in the Save panel (or File ▸ Audio Tags…) to add a title, reciter, album and cover picture — they're written into MP3, FLAC and OGG files.
  • Share: on a phone, ⇪ Share… sends the file straight to WhatsApp, Telegram, email… (on a computer it downloads instead).
  • Projects: File ▸ Save Project keeps every open tab with its name and markers, so you can carry on later.

Tutorials

Short, step-by-step lessons. Each one works on the whole file, or only on the part you select — select first when you want to change just one part.

Open, play and save a file

Beginner
  1. Press Open audio file (or File ▸ Open File…, Ctrl+O) and choose a file. You can also drag files from your computer onto the page.
  2. Press ▶ Play or Space. Press it again to pause. Click anywhere on the waveform to play from there.
  3. Zoom with + / − or Ctrl+mouse wheel; Fit (F) shows the whole file again.
  4. The overview bar above the waveform shows the whole file; the gold box is the part you're looking at. Drag the box to scroll, drag its edges to zoom in or out, click anywhere on it to jump there, double-click to see everything. (The mouse wheel scrolls too.)
  5. To listen slower or faster without changing the file, pick a speed in the 1× menu next to Loop.
  6. In the Save panel choose MP3 or WAV, then press Download (or Ctrl+S). To choose a new name first, use File ▸ Save File As….
Where does it go? Saved files go to your browser's Downloads folder.

Record from the microphone

Beginner
  1. Press ● Record (or R). The first time, your browser asks for permission to use the microphone — choose Allow.
  2. Speak or recite. The button shows the recording time.
  3. Press ■ Stop (or R again). If no file was open, the recording opens as a new file; otherwise it is inserted at the cursor (or replaces the selection).
  4. To record into a fresh file while others are open, press ＋ next to the tabs first, then Record.

Re-record one mistake (punch-in)

  1. Select the words you want to record again.
  2. Press ⟲● Re-record (next to Record), or right-click the selection ▸ Re-record Selection. The editor plays the 2 seconds before the selection so you can come in on time, then records.
  3. Press ■ Stop punch-in. Only the selection is replaced — with short fades so the joins don't click. Ctrl+Z brings the old take back.

Metronome and count-in

Control ▸ Recording Options… sets a count-in (1 or 2 bars of clicks before recording starts), a metronome while recording, the tempo, and how much plays before a punch-in. Use headphones so the clicks aren't recorded.

For a clean recording: hold the phone or microphone a hand's width away, record in a quiet room, and record a second of silence at the start — it is useful later for noise reduction.

Cut, copy, paste and delete (remove a section)

Beginner
  1. Select the part by dragging across the waveform. Shift+click extends a selection. For exact times, type them in the Start and End boxes.
  2. Adjust the selection: drag the gold bar along its bottom to slide the whole selection left or right (it keeps its length). Drag the left or right edge (the gold grips) to make it shorter or longer — the other edge stays where it is. Dragging past the side of the waveform scrolls along.
  3. Press Space to check what's selected. Turn on ⟲ Loop (L) to hear it repeat.
  4. Press Delete (Del) to remove it, or Cut (Ctrl+X) to remove it and keep a copy.
  5. To move it, click where it should go and Paste (Ctrl+V). Pasting over a selection replaces it.
  6. Made a mistake? Undo (Ctrl+Z) — as many times as you need. The History panel lets you jump back to any earlier step.
Keep only one part: select it and press Trim to selection (T).

Trim silence at the ends — or shorten long pauses

Beginner
  1. Open Edit ▸ Trim ▸ Auto Trim… (or the Auto trim… button).
  2. Choose what to trim: silence at the start and end, only the start, only the end — or start and end, and shorten long pauses inside.
  3. Count as silence below: −40 dB suits most recordings. If too much is cut, use a lower number (e.g. −50); if too little, a higher one (e.g. −35).
  4. Leave this much silence keeps a natural gap (250 ms by default).
  5. Press ▶ Preview to hear the first seconds, then Trim.

Split a recitation or lesson into lines

Popular
  1. Open the recording. In Markers & parts, press Auto-split at pauses. A red marker is placed in the middle of every pause, and the parts are listed underneath.
  2. Too many parts? Lower the silence level (e.g. −45 dB) or raise for at least (e.g. 600 ms). Too few? Do the opposite. Press Auto-split again after changing.
  3. Fix markers by hand: drag a marker to move it, press M to add one at the cursor, or ✕ in the list to remove one. ' and ; jump between markers.
  4. Press ▶ next to a part to check it.
  5. Name the parts (optional): type a name in each part's box, or press Name parts… and paste all the names at once, one per line (e.g. the ayah or line names). Names show on the waveform, stay with their parts when you edit, and are used for the saved files: al-fatiha-01-Bismillah.mp3…
  6. Press Download all parts (.zip) to save every part as a numbered file (name-01, name-02…), or ↓ to save one part. Edit ▸ Split File opens the parts as separate tabs instead.
Naming: the parts use the name in the file-name box above the waveform — change it before saving (e.g. al-fatiha → al-fatiha-01.mp3…).

Timestamps and "Edit in audio editor" links

Teaching
  1. Export timestamps: after splitting (and naming) the parts, press Timestamps… or Bookmark ▸ Export Timestamps: a text list, spreadsheet (CSV), JSON for a website or app, subtitles (SRT), lyrics (LRC) or Audacity labels.
  2. Import timestamps: Bookmark ▸ Import Timestamps… reads any of those files and puts the markers and names back — handy to reuse the same line timings.
  3. Open from a link: /audio-editor/?open=/path/to/file.mp3&name=Title opens that recording straight in the editor.
  4. Links on your pages: add <script src="/audio-editor/edit-link.js" defer></script> to a page and every audio player on it gets a small ✂ Edit in audio editor link (the Tafsīr page already has it).

Join several files into one

Popular
  1. Open File ▸ Join Audio Files… (or Join audio files on the start screen, or Join audio… in Tools).
  2. Open tabs are listed; press ＋ Add files… to add more from your computer.
  3. Tick what to include and put it in order with ↑ ↓.
  4. Optional: a pause between files (e.g. 1 s), or a crossfade to blend each file into the next (when the pause is 0). Leave Put a marker at each join on to find the joins later.
  5. Press Join. The result opens in a new tab — the originals are not changed.

Fade in and fade out

Beginner
  1. Quickest: drag the small square at the top-left of the waveform to the right — the shaded curve and label show the fade length. Let go to apply. The top-right square does the fade out.
  2. With a selection, the squares sit on the selection's corners, so you can fade any part.
  3. Or select the part and press Fade in / Fade out (I / O). Effects ▸ Fade In & Out Selection does both at once.
  4. Change the shape in Effects ▸ Fade Type: Linear, Smooth (default), Fast start or S-curve.

Make it louder, quieter or more even

Beginner
  1. Normalise (Shift+N) makes the loudest point just below the maximum — the safest way to make a quiet recording louder.
  2. Amplify… (Shift+A) changes the volume by a number of dB: +6 dB ≈ twice as loud, −6 dB ≈ half.
  3. Compressor… evens out loud and quiet parts, so a voice that fades in and out stays clear.
  4. Volume envelope (E): an orange line appears. Click it to add points, drag them up (louder, to 200%) or down (quieter, to silence). The waveform shows the result while you drag. Press Apply (Enter) — or Cancel (Esc).
Example: to lower background music under a voice, select the part with the voice, press E, drag the line down to 30%, Apply.

Clean up a voice recording (noise, hum, hiss)

Popular

The one-click way is Voice ▸ Voice Cleanup. For more control, do it in this order:

  1. Remove rumble… (Effects ▸ Clean up) cuts low hum, traffic and wind. 80–120 Hz suits speech.
  2. Noise reduction for steady hiss, fans or air-conditioning: select a second or so with only background noise (no voice), press Grab noise sample, press Esc to clear the selection, then Noise reduction…. Preview, adjust Reduce noise by, and apply.
  3. Noise gate… mutes whatever quiet noise is left between words.
  4. Click / Pop Removal… (Tools) smooths short clicks and mouth noises.
  5. Finish with Normalise.
AI noise removal (Effects ▸ AI Noise Removal…, or the button next to Noise reduction) needs no noise sample: a small neural network trained on speech (RNNoise) removes fans, traffic, keyboard and room noise and keeps the voice. It downloads once (5 MB) and then works offline. It is made for speech — for recitation with long held notes, preview it and lower the Amount if the voice sounds thin.
See the noise: View ▸ Spectrogram (or the Spectrogram button) shows the frequencies over time — low at the bottom, high at the top, brighter = louder. A steady hum is a bright horizontal line; hiss is a haze across the top; clicks are thin vertical lines. Select them there like on the waveform.
Don't overdo it: too much noise reduction makes a voice sound watery or robotic. Preview, and use the smallest amount that sounds clean.

Transcribe speech and edit the audio by editing the text

Speech
  1. Open Voice ▸ Transcribe & Edit by Text….
  2. Choose a model (Balanced is a good start; Fast for slow computers and phones) and the language, then Transcribe. The first time, the model downloads (40–250 MB); after that it works offline. Everything runs on your device.
  3. The text appears word by word. Click a word to hear it (and select it on the waveform); Shift+click another word to select everything in between.
  4. Delete selected words (or Del) cuts them out of the audio — and shortens the pause after them. Remove fillers removes every um / uh / er that is highlighted in yellow. Ctrl+Z undoes.
  5. Markers at sentences puts a named marker at each sentence, ready for Save parts. Export text… saves the transcript as plain text or subtitles (.srt, .vtt).
Speech recognition uses Whisper, which knows about 100 languages — including Arabic, English, Somali and Swahili — but not Afaan Oromoo yet. For Qur'an recitation the text is only a rough guide; never rely on it for the words of the Qur'an. If you edit the audio some other way, transcribe again so the words match.

Remove filler words (um, uh, er, ah)

Speech
  1. Open Voice ▸ Remove Filler Words (um, uh)…. The editor listens for hesitation sounds — a steady hum with a flat pitch and no consonants, usually between pauses.
  2. Each one found is listed with how sure the editor is. Press ▶ to hear it in context, and untick anything that is a real word.
  3. Not finding enough? Choose High sensitivity (and check more carefully). Too many? Choose Low.
  4. Choose Cut them out and close the gap (the pause left is shortened to about ¼ second) or Replace them with silence, then Remove ticked.
Not for Qur'an recitation: held vowels (madd) sound exactly like "um" and "ah" to the detector. It also can't recognise real words used as fillers ("like", "you know") — it listens to the sound, not the words.

Equaliser and sound effects

Intermediate
  1. Select a part (or nothing for the whole file) and open Effects ▸ Equalizer….
  2. Pick a preset — Voice clarity, Remove rumble / hum, Reduce hiss… — or drag the sliders / the dots on the graph. The shaded area shows your audio's own frequencies. Double-click a band to reset it.
  3. Press ▶ Preview: the audio loops and every change is heard immediately. Then Apply.
  4. Other effects work the same way — a small window with settings, ▶ Preview and Apply: Echo, Reverb, Compressor, Pitch, Tempo, Speed, Stereo Pan…
  5. Presets: in those windows, Save preset… keeps your settings under a name (e.g. “My recitation room”); pick it from the list next time.

Effect chains — your usual clean-up in one click

  1. Press Effect chain… (or Effects ▸ Effect Chains…).
  2. Start from a ready-made chain — Recitation polish, Clean voice, Phone recording fix, WhatsApp voice note, Slow down for memorising — or pick New empty chain.
  3. Change any step's numbers, reorder steps with ↑ ↓, remove with ✕, or add more with ＋ Add step. ▶ Preview plays the first 10 seconds with the whole chain.
  4. Save as… keeps it under your own name; then Apply. Saved chains also appear in Effects ▸ Apply Chain and the right-click menu.
Right-click (or press and hold on a phone) anywhere on the waveform for a menu of the common commands: play, cut, copy, paste, fades, effect chains, add a marker, re-record…

Slow down a recitation for memorising — tempo, pitch and speed

Popular
  1. Just listening slower? Use the 1× menu in the toolbar (0.5×–2×). The file is not changed.
  2. Save a slower version: Effects ▸ Tempo… and choose e.g. 80%. The voice keeps the same pitch.
  3. Repeat a line: select it and use Edit ▸ Repeat Loop… — e.g. 3 times with a 1-second pause, to recite along.
  4. A whole practice track: split the recitation into lines or ayat (Auto-split), then press Memorise… (Bookmark ▸ Memorisation Track…). Choose a pattern — each part repeated, linked (1, 2, 1+2, 3, 2+3…) or growing (1, 1–2, 1–3…) — how many times, and the pauses for reciting back. It opens as a new tab, with a marker and name for every block.
  5. Pitch… makes the voice higher or lower without changing the speed. Speed… changes both together, like a tape.

Work with several files — copy between them and mix

Intermediate
  1. Open several files (they become tabs), or press ＋ for an empty one. [ and ] switch tabs.
  2. Copy in one tab (Ctrl+C), switch tabs, click where it should go and paste (Ctrl+V). Different sample rates are converted automatically.
  3. Paste mix (Ctrl+Shift+V) plays the copied audio on top of what's there instead of inserting it.
  4. Mix with file… layers another tab or file at a chosen volume, from the cursor or the start — e.g. a voice over quiet background audio.
  5. Duplicate (Ctrl+D) opens a copy in a new tab, so you can try changes without touching the original.

Make a ringtone

Fun
  1. Select the part you want (or click where it should start).
  2. Open Tools ▸ Create Ringtone…, choose the length (30 s is usual) and the fades.
  3. Press Save ringtone. It is made loud and saved in the format chosen in the Save panel (MP3 works on most phones).

Key and beat detection

Music
  1. Tools ▸ Key Detector… shows the musical key (e.g. A minor), how sure it is, and a chart of which notes are used.
  2. To move the music to another key, choose it under Key changer and press Change key — the speed stays the same.
  3. Tools ▸ Automatic Beat Detection… finds the tempo in BPM. Check it with ▶ Play with click; if it's twice too fast or slow, press ½× or 2×.
  4. Press Add a marker on every beat (or every bar) to cut or loop exactly on the beat.
These are made for music with harmony and a steady rhythm. On speech or recitation there is no key or beat to find.

Multitrack editor — voice over background, and more

Intermediate

Put different recordings on separate tracks and play them together — e.g. a voice over quiet background audio, or a lesson built from several pieces.

  1. Open Tools ▸ Multitrack Editor… (Ctrl+M, or Multitrack on the start screen). The file you're editing becomes track 1.
  2. Add more audio: ＋ Track then pick an open tab in ＋ Add an open tab… (it goes on the selected track at the playhead), or Add files… (each file gets its own track).
  3. Arrange: drag a clip left or right — or up/down onto another track. It snaps to the start, the playhead and other clips' edges. Drag a clip's left or right edge to trim it.
  4. Edit clips: click a clip to select it, then Split at the playhead (S), Duplicate (Ctrl+D) or Delete (Del). Edit clip opens it in the normal editor as a new tab.
  5. Mix: each track has Vol (0–200%), Pan (left–right), M (mute) and S (solo — hear only this track). Changes are heard live while playing (Space).
  6. Volume and fades per clip: double-click a clip (or Volume & fades…) to make just that clip quieter or louder and give it a fade in / out. You can also drag the small squares at a selected clip's top corners.
  7. Several clips at once: Shift+click clips to select several (Ctrl+A selects all), then drag one to move them together, or Delete / Duplicate them.
  8. Markers and looping: M (or ◆ Marker) marks the playhead; ; and ' jump between marks; drag a mark on the ruler to move it. Drag along the ruler to choose a part to loop, and L (or ⟲ Loop) turns looping on and off.
  9. ● Record track (R) records a new track from the microphone while the other tracks play — use headphones so they aren't recorded too. The editor moves the new take earlier to make up for the delay in your device; if takes still land late or early, change recording delay in Control ▸ Recording Options….
  10. Mix down → new tab turns everything into one stereo file in the editor (save it as MP3 from there), or Save mix downloads it straight away.
Closing the multitrack (✕ or Esc) keeps your session — open it again to carry on. Save project keeps it for another day. Undo / redo (Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y) work inside it too.

Text to speech

Speech
  1. Open Voice ▸ Text to Speech… (also in Tools, and Text to speech on the start screen). The first time, the speech engine (18 MB) downloads — after that it's cached and works offline.
  2. Type or paste the text. Arabic is written right-to-left automatically.
  3. Choose the voice: Afaan Oromoo, Arabic, English and 130+ other languages; male or female; the speed (words per minute), pitch and pause between words.
  4. Press ▶ Preview to listen.
  5. Press Insert at cursor to add it to the open file (it replaces the selection, if any), or New tab to open it as its own file — then save it as MP3 like any recording.
The voice is synthetic (a little robotic). It's great for announcements, prompts, spelling out names and pronunciation practice — not a replacement for a real reciter.

Convert or clean up many files at once

Intermediate
  1. Open File ▸ Batch Converter… and choose all the files.
  2. Choose MP3 or WAV, and what to do to each: trim silent ends, normalise, convert to mono, fade in / out.
  3. Press Convert. All the results are saved together in one .zip. Your open tabs are not changed.

Autosave, offline use and installing the app

Beginner
  1. Autosave: a few seconds after each change, your open files (with markers and tags) and the multitrack session are kept in this browser. If the page closes, the phone restarts or the battery runs out, open the editor again and press Restore my work. Turn it off in File ▸ Autosave in This Browser (e.g. on a shared computer).
  2. Install as an app: press ⤓ Install app at the top (or Help ▸ Install as an App…). It then opens from your home screen or desktop like a normal app. On iPhone/iPad: Safari's Share button → Add to Home Screen.
  3. Offline: after your first visit the editor works without internet. Text to speech and M4A saving work offline after you've used them once.
Autosave lives only in this browser on this device. To move work to another device, or keep it for a long time, use Save Project.

Language, dark mode, phones and your own shortcuts

Settings
  • Language: pick English, Afaan Oromoo or العربية at the top right (or View ▸ Language). Arabic turns the page right-to-left; the timeline still runs left to right. Menus, buttons and dialogs are translated; this help page and some messages are still in English. The Afaan Oromoo wording is new — tell us if a word should be better.
  • Dark mode: the ◐ button at the top switches light and dark. View ▸ Theme also has Automatic, which follows your phone or computer.
  • Phones and tablets: pinch with two fingers on the waveform (or the multitrack timeline) to zoom in and out, and move your fingers sideways to scroll. Press and hold for the quick menu. Buttons, handles and marker grips are bigger on touch screens.
  • Your own shortcuts: Help ▸ Keyboard Shortcuts… lists every menu command. Press Change and then the key you want (with Ctrl, Alt or Shift if you like); ↺ puts the built-in key back. Your keys are shown in the menus.
  • Keyboard only: Tab to the menu bar, ↓ or Enter opens a menu, ↑ ↓ move, → opens a submenu, ← / Esc go back, ← → on the menu bar switch menus. Screen readers hear the menu items, whether they are ticked or unavailable, and messages at the bottom of the screen.

Save your work and continue later (projects)

Intermediate
  1. File ▸ Save Project saves every open tab — audio, names and markers — and the multitrack session (tracks, volumes, pans, mute/solo and every clip's position and trim) in one …-project.zip file. The multitrack editor has its own Save project button too.
  2. Later, File ▸ Open Project… (or just open that .zip) brings all the tabs back exactly as they were.
  3. Projects are lossless, so saving and reopening never lowers the quality.
Remember: nothing is kept on a server. If you close the page without saving, your changes are gone. The browser warns you if a tab has unsaved changes.

Questions & troubleshooting

The page looks old or a new feature is missing

Your browser may be showing a saved copy. Press Ctrl+Shift+R (Mac: Cmd+Shift+R). On a phone, close the tab and open the page again.

Recording doesn't start

Allow microphone access when the browser asks. If you said no before, click the lock icon next to the address and allow the microphone, then reload.

Nothing plays

Check the volume and that the tab isn't muted. On iPhone, turn off the silent switch.

No sound on iPhone or iPad

Check the silent switch on the side of the iPhone — when it is on, Safari mutes web audio. Turn it off (or use headphones), then tap ▶ again. After a phone call, tap anywhere once so Safari lets the page play sound again.

My file won't open

Most MP3, WAV, M4A and OGG files work. Some protected or unusual files (e.g. WMA in some browsers) don't — convert them first, or try another browser such as Chrome or Edge.

Large files are slow

Everything runs on your device, so an hour-long file needs a lot of memory. Split long recordings first, or work on a selection.

Where are my files saved?

In your browser's Downloads folder. Recent files are remembered in this browser only; clearing your browsing data removes them.

Is it really private?

Yes. The editor is a web page that works entirely inside your browser. Your audio is never sent anywhere.
