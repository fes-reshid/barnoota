          Guide & shortcuts Full help & tutorials ↗

          Getting started

          • The menu bar at the top (File, Edit, Effects, Control, Tools, Bookmark, View, Voice, Help) has every command, like WavePad; shortcuts are shown next to each item. The panels below the waveform have the most-used ones as buttons — hide them with View ▸ Show Effects & Tools Panels for a cleaner screen.
          • Open… loads one or more audio files; each gets its own tab. You can also drag files onto the page.
          • The + next to the tabs starts an empty file: record into it, paste audio copied from another tab, or drop a file on it.
          • Click the waveform to place the cursor, drag to select. Effects apply to the selection — or to the whole file when nothing is selected.
          • Volume envelope (E): an orange line appears over the selection (or whole file). Click the line to add points and drag them up (louder, up to 200%) or down (quieter, down to silence); the waveform shows the result as you go. Double-click or right-click a point to remove it, ▶ Preview to listen, then Apply.
          • Move or resize a selection: drag the gold bar along the bottom of the selection to slide it left or right (same length). Drag either edge to make it longer or shorter — the other edge stays where it is.
          • Text to speech (Voice or Tools menu, or the start screen): type text, pick a language such as Afaan Oromoo, Arabic or English, press ▶ Preview, then insert it at the cursor or open it as a new tab.
          • Multitrack editor (Tools ▸ Multitrack Editor, Ctrl+M): put audio on several tracks and play them together — e.g. a voice over quiet background audio. Drag clips along the timeline or to another track, drag clip edges to trim, split at the playhead (S), set each track's volume, pan, mute and solo, clip volume and fades (double-click a clip), Shift+click to move several clips, markers (M), a loop (drag on the ruler, L), or ● Record track while the others play. Mix down turns it into one file.
          • Effect chains, presets and punch-in: Effect chain… applies several effects in one step (ready-made clean-ups or your own); effect windows can save presets; ⟲● Re-record records over just the selection after a short pre-roll; Control ▸ Recording Options adds a count-in and metronome. Right-click (or long-press) the waveform for a quick menu.
          • Overview bar (above the waveform): the whole file in miniature. The gold box is the part shown below — drag it to scroll, drag its edges to zoom in or out, click anywhere to jump there, double-click to see everything. Turn it off in View ▸ Overview Bar.
          • Fade handles: the two small squares at the top corners of the selection (or of the whole file) — drag the left one to the right to fade in, the right one to the left to fade out. The shaded curve shows the fade and its length while you drag.

          Combining two recordings

          1. Open both files — they appear as tabs.
          2. In the first tab, select the part you want and press Ctrl+C.
          3. Switch tabs, click where it should go and press Ctrl+V to insert it, or Ctrl+Shift+V to play it on top of what's there.
          4. Mix with file… layers a whole tab or file at a chosen volume — e.g. a voice over quiet background audio.

          Splitting a recitation into lines

          1. Press Auto-split at pauses. A red marker is placed in the middle of every pause.
          2. Too many parts? Lower the threshold (e.g. −45 dB) or raise the minimum pause length. Too few? Do the opposite.
          3. Drag markers to adjust them, press M to add one, or ✕ in the list to remove one.
          4. Press ▶ next to a part to check it, then Download all parts for a zip of numbered files.

          Effects & tools

          • Effects with … open a small window: set the values, press ▶ Preview to hear up to 8 seconds, then Apply.
          • Noise reduction (like WavePad): select a second or two where there is only background noise — no voice — and press Grab noise sample. Then clear the selection (Esc) and press Noise reduction…; preview, and raise or lower the amount. Without a sample it guesses the noise from the quietest parts.
          • Clean up a recording: Remove rumble (hum and wind) → Noise reduction (steady hiss) → Noise gate (anything left between words) → Normalise.
          • Repeat loop repeats the selected line several times, with an optional pause — useful for memorising.
          • Auto trim… cuts the silence at the start and end, or shortens every long pause inside the recording to a length you choose — great for tightening a recitation or a lecture.
          • Split into tabs opens each marked part (or each side of the cursor) as a separate tab. Join audio… does the opposite: tick open tabs and/or add files from your computer, put them in order with ↑ ↓, choose a pause or a crossfade between them, and they're joined into a new tab (also on the start screen as Join audio files).
          • Batch convert processes many files at once — e.g. convert a folder of WAV recordings to normalised MP3s.
          • Equaliser… is a 10-band graphic EQ like WavePad's: drag the sliders or the dots on the graph (double-click resets a band), or pick a preset such as Voice clarity or Remove rumble / hum. The shaded area shows your audio's own frequencies. ▶ Preview loops the audio so you hear every change live.
          • Voice ▸ Remove Filler Words finds hesitation sounds (um, uh, er, ah) in speech, lets you listen to each one and untick real words, then cuts them out (closing the gap) or silences them. It listens to the sound, not the words, so it works in any language — but not on recitation, where held vowels sound like fillers.
          • Tools ▸ Key Detector tells you the musical key (e.g. A minor) and shows which notes are used; Key Changer moves the audio to another key without changing its speed. Tools ▸ Automatic Beat Detection finds the tempo in BPM — check it with ▶ Play with click, fix a double/half tempo with ½× / 2×, and add a marker on every beat or bar. Both work on the selection, or on the whole file.
          • Pitch… makes a voice higher or lower while keeping the same speed (+12 semitones = one octave up). Tempo… makes it faster or slower while keeping the same voice — e.g. 80% to slow a recitation down for learning. Speed… changes both together, like a tape.
          • The playback speed menu (1×) only changes how you listen; use Tempo… or Speed… to change the file itself.
          • History lists every change; click any line to go back to that point.

          Saving

          • Choose MP3 (small, good for sharing) or WAV (lossless, bigger). 192 kbps MP3 is a good default.
          • A * on a tab means it has changes you haven't downloaded yet.
          • Everything stays on your device — nothing is uploaded — so download your work before closing the page.

            Text to speech

            Type or paste text and it is turned into speech — right here in your browser, in more than 100 languages including Afaan Oromoo, Arabic and English. The voice is synthetic (robot-like), good for announcements, prompts and pronunciation practice.

            The speech engine (18 MB) downloads the first time you use it, then stays cached.

            Remove filler words

            Finds hesitation sounds — um, uh, er, ah, mm — by how they sound: a steady hum with a flat pitch and no consonants, usually between pauses. Works in any language. Listen to each one (▶) and untick anything that is a real word. Not for Qur'an recitation: long held vowels (madd) sound just like fillers.
